About

Built from patterns across hundreds of restaurants

Restaurant Casestudy exists because the same restaurants keep failing for the same reasons — and almost none of those reasons are the food.

Sixty percent of restaurants close within their first year. Ninety percent within five. Nine out of ten — savings gone, families disappointed, a lease still running.

But nobody stops to ask why. Because if ninety percent are failing, there must be common mistakes. And if there are common mistakes, there are common solutions — because on the other side of those statistics stand restaurants that have run for twenty, thirty, fifty years. Packed every evening. Loved by their neighbourhoods. Passed down through generations.

They did something right. This product is an attempt to write down exactly what.

The core idea. Most restaurant failures are decided long before opening night — in a rent number nobody stress-tested, a menu nobody costed, a reserve that was spent on interiors. Those are all things a worksheet can catch, if somebody asks the right question at the right moment.

Who is behind it

Atul Sharma is a marketer who has worked closely with restaurants of every size — small QSRs tucked into busy lanes, mid-sized casual dining rooms, full-scale fine dining. Different cuisines, different cities, different price points.

Across all of them the same patterns kept appearing. The same mistakes. The same blind spots. The same missed opportunities. Every tool here is one of those patterns, turned into a question with a number attached.

“What draws me in is the energy. A well-run restaurant is one of the most alive places on earth. Every second something is happening — orders flying, flavours being created, strangers becoming regulars, memories being made over a plate of food.”

And, like most people who use this, he wants to open a restaurant himself one day. So in many ways the product is built as much for its author as for its users — everything he would want to know before making that leap.

How we think about your data

Your rent, your margins and your break-even are among the most sensitive numbers you have. So they never leave your browser. There is no server holding your P&L, because there is no server holding it. The trade-off is that your work does not sync across devices — which is why every tool pushes you to export.
